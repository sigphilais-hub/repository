<p align="center"><strong>SIGPHIL THEORIZING REPOSITORY</strong></p>



This repository is an initiative of the AIS Special Interest Group on Philosophy (SIGPhil) and emerges from a collective recognition that the vitality of the Information Systems (IS) field depends upon its capacity to engage deeply with theory, theorizing, and the diverse philosophical traditions that sustain them. In an era where technological and organizational transformations evolve at an unprecedented pace, it is increasingly vital to revisit and reconnect with the theoretical foundations that give meaning and coherence to our research endeavors, so we may make sense of this changing world and act in more informed and effective ways.

This repository is grounded in the belief that progress in IS research requires open dialogue, theoretical reflection, and a pluralism of thought. It does not aim to impose a singular worldview or methodological orthodoxy but instead to create a scholarly space where different approaches, paradigms, and philosophies can coexist, interact, and challenge one another. Such intellectual diversity, we believe, is not a weakness but a defining strength of the IS community.

By embracing multiple perspectives, we encourage critical reflection and foster richer, more nuanced understandings of the phenomena that shape our daily lives and of concerns for members of our field. The repository is designed as a living, collaborative initiative; one that evolves alongside the community it serves. It aspires to be both a resource and a forum: a place to discover, to debate, and to contribute.

Through the collection and curation of theoretical resources and illustrative examples, this platform seeks to support IS students, researchers, and practitioners in exploring the nature of theory and the process of theorizing. It also aims to make visible the often tacit philosophical assumptions and knowledge that underpin our work, inviting renewed attention to how theory is conceived, developed, and applied.

We warmly invite members of the IS community to explore, reflect, and participate in the continued development of this repository, not merely as consumers of knowledge, but as co-creators of a shared intellectual resource.

To facilitate navigation and engagement, the repository is organized into several interconnected sections, each addressing a key dimension of theorizing and its practice in the IS field:

1. **Philosophical Underpinnings of Theorizing**  
   This section explores the philosophical assumptions that inform theorizing in IS. It introduces diverse schools of thought and their implications for how we conceptualize theory and theorizing.

2. **Overview of Theorizing and the Theorizing Process**  
   Here, the focus is on the dynamics of theorizing itself: how we may develop theories and make sense of our reality.

3. **Products of Theorizing**  
   This section examines the various products of theorizing that come into play before, during, and after the theorizing process.

4. **Key IS Topics and Related Theoretical Efforts**  
   This section examines the ways in which theoretical perspectives shape and guide specific domains of IS research.

5. **Theoretical Borrowing**  
   We frequently engage with theories originating from reference disciplines. This section examines the reasons and approaches behind this practice and presents examples of thoughtful and successful theoretical borrowing.

6. **Managerial Practices and Related Philosophical Foundations**  
   This section aims to bridge theory and practice by discussing the philosophical commitments and theoretical insights that underpin current managerial ideas and practices.

7. **Empirical Help**  
   Recognizing the interdependence of theory and empirical work, this section offers practical resources such as open datasets and secondary data sources to support theory-driven research.

Finally, a **Discussion and Issues** section provides an open forum for dialogue, reflection, and critique. It is intended as a space where ideas can be tested, assumptions revisited, and new directions for theorizing can emerge through community engagement.
